The Culinary Exchange.

A Storyline web activity built by a member of my instructional design team under my leadership and review. I did not build this object. I am including it because it shows the kind of interactive learning work that can come from a team when the design process, review expectations, and support structure are working.

Ownership note: This activity was built by a member of my team. My role was leadership, review, and support, not direct development of the Storyline file.

Build context: Articulate Storyline, variables, layers, quiz mechanics, and a web-published activity that could be configured for SCORM score passback.

What was happening

One part of instructional design management is making sure good work can happen without the manager building every asset. That means setting expectations, reviewing the work, asking useful questions, and helping the team make decisions that support the learner and the course goal.

What the team built

The activity uses several Storyline mechanics in one learner-facing object: variables, layers, and a quiz structure that could be connected to SCORM reporting for score passback. It is useful as a portfolio example because it shows more than static presentation work. It shows interactive development decisions inside a tool our team uses for course activities.

What it demonstrates

Articulate Storyline Team-produced learning object Variables Layers Quiz mechanics SCORM-ready design

My role

My contribution was management and review: supporting the team member's development work, helping keep the interaction aligned with the learning purpose, and reviewing the activity as part of the team's production process. I want to be clear about that boundary. This is not here as my solo build. It is here as an example of the type of work my team can produce under the processes and expectations I help maintain.
