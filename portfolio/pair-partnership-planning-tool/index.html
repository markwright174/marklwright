Team enablement / ID operations

Pair Partnership Planning Tool.

A lightweight operating model for pair-based instructional design work. It helps design partners share context, review work, plan coverage, surface concerns early, and grow professionally without turning collaboration into a rigid compliance process.

This public version keeps the operating model while removing live staff notes, program-specific details, and institution-specific references.

What was happening

Pairing instructional designers can make course work more resilient, but only if the partnership has enough structure to be useful. Without that structure, one person may hold too much project history, coverage becomes difficult, and concerns show up late. With too much structure, collaboration starts to feel like a form instead of a professional relationship.

What I built

I built a practical planning tool for pair-based instructional design work. It defines the partnership roles, core norms, minimum check-in cadence, shared note templates, a supplemental tracker model, and a concern path for moving from ambiguity to action before a small issue becomes a larger project problem.

What it demonstrates

My role

I shaped the operating model around the practical needs of an instructional design team: continuity, quality, transparency, and trust. The goal was to make partnership easier to practice without making it feel like surveillance or a compliance exercise. A useful system should make good collaboration more likely, not make people spend all their energy proving collaboration happened.
