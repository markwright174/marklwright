Experience

I have usually worked where learning meets friction.

The settings have changed: classrooms, faculty support, LMS work, curriculum projects, STEM programs, and learning operations. The shape of the work has been surprisingly consistent. Something complex needs to become teachable, usable, accessible, and maintainable for the people living with it every day.

Patterns in the work

Curriculum systems Instructional design management Faculty support Project coordination Learning platforms Accessibility

Representative work

Curriculum architecture

Helping proficiency-based education become real course work

One of the harder parts of curriculum change is that the idea can sound clean while the actual course work is anything but. My role has included helping deconstruct curriculum, align competencies and objectives, prioritize development work, and keep the transition connected to what programs and learners actually need.

Team and process leadership

Keeping instructional design work moving without losing the people

I manage instructional design projects across large course portfolios and help coordinate the people attached to them: designers, faculty, program leadership, and other stakeholders. The point is not process for its own sake. The point is making sure quality, decisions, timelines, accessibility, and human attention can all stay in the room at the same time.

Learning technology

Making platforms easier to teach and easier to use

I have worked inside Blackboard, D2L Brightspace, Canvas, Codio, YuJa, and other learning tools from both the support side and the design side. That has included platform migration, faculty training, tool evaluation, accessibility review, and the quiet work of making the system less confusing than it was yesterday.

Faculty and educator support

Building resources people can return to

Live training matters, but people also need something useful after the meeting ends. I have built self-paced teaching certification work, internal training sites, support resources, and guidance for faculty, staff, students, and educators who needed a clearer path through online teaching and learning systems.

Earlier foundations

Classrooms, STEM programs, and education services

Before much of my current systems work, I taught young learners and adults, directed tutoring-program work, managed STEM education programming, and led education services operations. That earlier work still matters. It keeps me honest about what happens when a design meets a teacher, a learner, a schedule, and a room full of real constraints.

What I carry forward

The through line

Good learning work is not just content. It is structure, timing, trust, usability, and support. I tend to notice the places where those pieces stop lining up, then build ways to make the work visible enough to fix.

The practical tools

My toolkit includes instructional design, curriculum development, project coordination, LMS-supported delivery, accessibility review, quality assurance, documentation, stakeholder communication, and a careful use of AI where it helps people think, review, organize, or find patterns.

Formal background

My formal education includes a master's in Educational Technology and a bachelor's in English. I am also a QM Peer Reviewer and hold a Digital Accessibility certification.
